The WAOW 1 Museum

Before WAOW 2, there was WAOW.

A dream drawn on paper.
Four years in the making.
Seven years exploring Indonesia.
This is where the WAOW story began.
2012 — 2018

It started
with a dream

WAOW began in the imagination of Michel Deville.
His idea was to create a traditional Indonesian sailing boat built around one thing he deeply loved: diving.
Michel drew the first plans and surrounded himself with people who could turn that vision into something real.
What started on paper would become a 60-metre wooden sailing boat built to explore Indonesia.

Four years
in the making

WAOW was built over four years by Haji Wahab and his team of Indonesian shipwrights, using generations of traditional boatbuilding knowledge.
Inspired by the Indonesian phinisi and combined with the lines of a schooner, she brought together traditional craftsmanship and a boat designed specifically for long journeys and diving.
60 metres · 3 masts · 900 m² of sails · 850+ tonnes

Why “WAOW”?

During the design and construction, one reaction kept coming back whenever people discovered the project:
“WAOW.”
The name stayed.
It would later take on another meaning:

WATER ADVENTURE OCEAN WIDE

A name that turned out to fit rather well.

Seven years at sea

INDONESIA, ONE JOURNEY AT A TIME.
In April 2012, WAOW welcomed her first guests.
Over the years, she sailed through some of Indonesia's most remote waters — from Komodo and Raja Ampat to the Banda Sea, Forgotten Islands, Triton Bay, Halmahera, Cenderawasih and beyond.
Twenty guests at a time.
Long days underwater.
Thousands of dives.
Countless miles at sea.
And a community that grew with every journey.

01.02.2018

The day WAOW
was lost

During the night of January 31 to February 1, 2018, WAOW caught fire while in the port of Biak, Indonesia.
She was lost to the sea.
Everyone on board made it to safety.
After years of construction, thousands of miles and seven years of journeys across Indonesia, the first chapter of WAOW came to an end.
But not the story.

The story wasn't

WAOW lived on in the people who built her, worked aboard her, travelled on her and grew up with her.
Years later, that same spirit would return to the shipyard.
Not to rebuild WAOW I.
To begin the next chapter.
